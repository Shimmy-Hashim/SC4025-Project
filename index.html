        How the authenticity check works

        The check combines five factors about each seller's offer into a band. It is guidance to help you decide, not a guarantee.

        What it looks at

        • Seller history: time selling and feedback (seller performance data)
        • Brand authorisation: whether the brand lists the seller as authorised (Brand Registry)
        • Price against other sellers: offers far below others on the same listing
        • Review patterns: reviews mentioning fakes, wrong items or mismatched descriptions, with near-duplicates removed
        • Past complaints: authenticity complaints in the last 90 days

        How much each factor counts

        Direct reports from buyers count most. Signals that can have innocent explanations count less. The team set these shares using swing weighting, a structured method from UK government decision-analysis guidance. They are the team's judgement, not a measured accuracy.

          Serious alerts come first

          Some findings are shown in a red alert and always give a Low band, whatever the other factors say: a price far below every other seller, a seller claiming authorisation the brand denies, or a counterfeit confirmed by an investigation. Missing information never counts against a seller.

          What the bands mean

            What it cannot tell you

            A High band does not prove a product is genuine, and a Low band does not prove it is fake. Keep reading reviews and seller details, especially for expensive items.

            When there is not enough data

            Offers from sellers with fewer than 20 reviews or under 90 days of history are not scored. A serious alert is still shown for them.

            If a seller disagrees

            Sellers can dispute any factor. A person reviews the dispute before the score changes.
